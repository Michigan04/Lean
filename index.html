Every break was announced in public. None of the firms had a buffer that outlasted the time to fix, and in each case that buffer would have cost ~10× less than the break did

Warning lead time ran from 22 months (IndiGo) down to 29 days (Nexperia). It decided how much could be done, not whether the operation survived. Survival came down to one inequality: time-to-survive on buffer + warning time actually used ≥ time-to-recover.18

3Three different failure modes, one common pattern
Saw it, didn't act

IndiGo had 22 months, longer than the 12-month hiring cycle. The failure was a decision: growth was prioritised over crew. A warning system alone would not have saved it.

Too little time

EV makers had 12 weeks against a ~4-month fix and 4 weeks of stock. Only buffer held before the warning, or a pre-qualified substitute, could bridge it.

No time at all

Carmakers had 29 days. Mahindra coped because the quarter was already covered and substitutes existed [C5]. Dual-qualification was the only lever that worked.

Pattern: a single point of failure + a public signal + a buffer measured in weeks against a fix measured in months. Lean KPIs (inventory days, crew utilisation) rewarded each firm for removing exactly the slack that mattered.

Electric two-wheelers: a month of magnets costs Rs 1.6 cr a year to hold, and a month without them costs Rs 24 cr. Hold 4 months and pre-qualify a second motor

Representative Bajaj-scale maker at 22,500 scooters a month. A buffer month pays for itself if a curb longer than that buffer hits more often than once in 16 years. China imposed two magnet-relevant curbs in 2025 (April, October), and the October one is only paused.13

390-day playbookKPI: lost scooters <3,000 a month through any curb
Amber posture · now

Raise cover from 4 to 6 months with Tier-1 call-offs. Pre-file licences with end-use certificates [12]. Freeze changes to the magnet spec. Brief the board on the Rs 15 cr working-capital ask.

Day 0–7 · contain

War room: COO plus procurement, R&D, finance, sales. Count every magnet owned, at Tier-1s and in transit. Place maximum licensed orders. Tell the testing agency a motor switch is coming.

Day 8–30 · allocate

Send scarce magnets to the highest-contribution variants. Move low-power trims to light-REE magnets (no Dy/Tb), as Bajaj did by Aug 2025.5 Activate ex-China suppliers in Japan, Korea and Vietnam.

Day 31–60 · switch

Ramp the pre-homologated ferrite/light-REE motor to 60% of volume. Re-plan dealer allocation. Give booked customers delivery dates so the demand defers to us instead of moving to rivals.

Day 61–90 · rebuild

Refill cover to target before relaxing. Re-set trigger levels from this event's data. Sign offtake MoUs with REPM bidders so domestic supply arrives with a buyer.

A1 · Model details and calculations

aFormulas
h = Q × m × c = 22,500 × 3,450 × 20% = Rs 1.55 cr / buffer-month / yr
L = lost units × contribution = 10,000 × 24,420 = Rs 24.4 cr / month
E[loss | B] = p × Σ P(D=d) × max(0, d − B) × L × f
Total(B) = B × h + E[loss | B] (+ programme cost)
Add month B+1 while p × P(D > B) × L ≥ h
f = share of volume not covered by the alternative motor (1.0 for A and B, 0.4 for C)
bWhy these risk inputs
p = ⅓: magnet-relevant Chinese moves in Dec 2023 (tech ban)20, Apr 2025 [B1] and Oct 202513, i.e. three in ~24 months. We take one supply-cutting curb every 3 years to stay conservative.
Length: India waited ~4 months for relief in 2025 [B10], and most licences were still pending in October.12 The 9–12 month tail stands for the Oct-2025 rules (incl. the 0.1% content rule) coming back into force.
Lost units: 100% treated as lost (rivals TVS and Ather gained share in Aug 2025).6 If half were only deferred, L halves, break-even p becomes 1 in 8 years, and optimal cover is still 4 months.
